# Apex Fleet Consulting — Inbound Assessment Routing Matrix

How a Fleet Maintenance Exposure Assessment submission becomes a routed, scored, priced next
step inside one business day — without quoting an Apex fee on a deal that belongs to SVT. This is
the live, built front door: the assessment form, the day-one reply, and the Microsoft Bookings
discovery call page. `prospecting`'s job is to drive traffic into this machinery, not duplicate it —
see `SKILL.md` §10.

Form: **Fleet Maintenance Exposure Assessment** · promised reply: **within 1 business day**.
Last confirmed live/working: 27 Aug 2026 (test booking). Assessment form ID: 06797695.

## Step 0 — Route it before pricing it

An assessment submission is an inbound fleet opportunity — it goes through the routing gates
before any reply is written. Quoting an Apex fee on an account that should have been an SVT
objective costs real money and creates an attribution argument later.

**Run `lead-routing` on the submission before writing the reply.** It answers, in order: who
delivers — SVT or Apex — and, only if Apex, whether this gets registered with Uptech. Log the
deciding gate whichever way it goes (Step 5).

The assessment form captures the two fields the gates need directly: state(s) of operation →
footprint (Gate 1); fleet size → tier test.

- **Gate 1 — Footprint.** SVT centres: Anaheim, Corona, Hesperia, Escondido, San Diego, North Las
  Vegas. Mobile-only: Greater Phoenix, Northern California. Outside those with no nearby cluster to
  carry two techs and a manager → Apex. State the shortfall in technician-hours, not "out of area."
- **Gate 2 — Named Exhibit A objective?** Amazon DSP, Amazon corporate registration/meeting, or
  Enterprise PMA. Anything else → Apex. Fleet size alone is not a qualifier.
- **Gate 3 — Enterprise PMA only.** Needs 12+ month term AND $100K+/yr gross profit, both. Either
  missing → Apex, and say which failed.
- **Gate 4 — Is the tier worth routing?** A qualifying deal paying at the bottom of the table isn't
  a meaningful SVT opportunity.
- **Gate 5 — Closes before 31 Jan 2027?** Exhibit A pays only on objectives completed inside the
  term. No tail, no lookback.

**Most inbound web traffic fails Gate 1 or Gate 2 and lands Apex.** That's the expected outcome,
not a problem — but the gate still gets recorded either way.

**Uptech overlay — Apex lane only.** Register if any one is true: 500+ vehicles, multi-state/toll-
corridor operations, or an admitted citation burden. The form's fleet-size and state fields answer
the first two directly. Never register individual Amazon DSPs at 20–40 vans. Registration lasts 180
days — diary day 150 for renewal.

**Standing boundary, every Apex deliverable:** toll and citation are a findings category, never a
vendor recommendation. Quantify exposure freely; name no toll-management vendor other than Uptech
(and only in the ways `lead-routing`/Uptech rules already permit). VNO and FTI need the explicit
scope carve-out in the engagement letter.

## Step 1 — Score the seven

Count every answer that is not "Yes." Treat "Not sure" as a "No" — if the fleet manager can't
answer it, the system doesn't exist in usable form, and that itself is the finding.

| Not-Yes count | Band | What it means | How you open the reply |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Contained | A real program with one or two specific gaps. Not the audit case. | Name the specific gap and price the single service that closes it. Don't pitch the full audit — oversold and they'll know it. |
| 3–4 | Meaningful exposure | The whitepaper's own threshold. Reactive maintenance is costing more than their data shows. | Lead with the two biggest gaps and what each typically costs. The audit pays for itself. |
| 5–7 | No baseline | They can't answer basic questions about their own spend. Every number they have is unreliable. | Don't itemize seven failures — reads as an indictment. Name the two that cost the most and say the rest follow from them. |

## Step 2 — Each gap points at one service

Four of the seven questions are the four areas of the Fleet Maintenance Audit. Two are Vendor
Network Optimization. One is program design. This mapping is what makes the recommendation
defensible rather than a guess.

| Q | A "No" means | Service line | Proposal to send |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No documented PM schedule | PM program design | Too small alone — fold into Audit + Optimization, or Operations Assessment |
| 2 | Can't report PM compliance | Audit — Area 2, PM Compliance Review | Fleet Maintenance Audit |
| 3 | Doesn't know cost per vehicle | Audit — Area 1, Maintenance Spend Analysis | Fleet Maintenance Audit |
| 4 | No negotiated labor rates | Vendor Network Optimization | Vendor Optimization |
| 5 | Downtime not root-caused | Audit — Area 4, Downtime & Availability | Fleet Maintenance Audit |
| 6 | No preferred vendor network | Vendor Network Optimization | Vendor Optimization |
| 7 | Spend not forecast or tracked | Audit — Area 1, plus ongoing oversight | Audit, then Advisory Retainer |

**Decision rule:**

| Pattern | Lead with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Two or more of Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7 are No | Fleet Maintenance Audit | No baseline. Cheapest way in — $8,000 floor. |
| Q4 and Q6 both No, audit questions mostly clean | Vendor Network Optimization | Skip the audit. Fastest measurable payback sold — 8–18% vendor spend reduction, cost recovered in 60–90 days. |
| Five or more No across the board | Audit + Optimization Program | Diagnosis and fix in one engagement, $25,000. They can't implement recommendations alone — nothing to measure against exists. |
| Only Q1 is No | Operations Assessment or retainer | PM program design alone doesn't clear a fee floor. Scope it up or hold for a retainer conversation. |

Diagnose before designing. Price one phase at a time — never the whole roadmap in the first reply.

## Step 3 — Size it from the form answers

Both playbooks score five dimensions, 1–3 each. The assessment answers most of them directly.
Score, total, read the band — then hold the number until the discovery call confirms it.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fleet Size | Fleet size dropdown | <100 | 100–250 | 250–500+ |
| Asset Mix | Ask on the call | Single class | 2–3 classes | 3+ or specialty |
| Data Quality | Q2 + Q3 answers | Both Yes | One Yes | Both No / Not sure |
| Vendor Footprint | Vendor count + locations | 1–5, single site | 6–15 or multi-site | 15+, regional |
| Deliverables | Your scope call | Summary findings | Full written report | Report + roadmap + action plan |

| Total | Indicated range | Typical profile |
|---|---|---|
| 5–7 | $8,000–$11,000 | Small fleet, single location, clean data, summary report |
| 8–10 | $11,000–$15,000 | Mid-size fleet, mixed assets, regional vendors |

**Where the form's buckets don't line up:** the assessment offers Under 50 / 50–150 / 150–500 /
500+; both playbooks band at 100 and 250. "50–150" straddles Score 1 and 2; "150–500" straddles 2
and 3. Get the actual vehicle count on the call before a number goes in a proposal — quote a range,
never a figure, until then.

## Step 4 — The day-one reply

The acknowledgment already went out on submit. This is the promised reply — and the email *is* the
report. Don't build a PDF: costs a day, and the goal is a booked call, not a document. Send from
`brooke@apexfleetconsulting.com`, never the SVT address.

| Beat | Job it does | Length |
|---|---|---|
| The score | States the number plainly. No drama, no grading. | 1 sentence |
| What it means | Translates the number into their situation, normalized so it reads as diagnosis not indictment. | 2–3 sentences |
| The two gaps | Each with a published benchmark attached — the only part that proves industry knowledge. | 1 short paragraph each |
| Sequence | What you'd do first, and why that order. Judgment, not a menu. | 2 sentences |
| The ask | 30 minutes, explicitly no-pitch, valuable either way. | 1 sentence + booking link |

### Worked example

Scored from a real submission: 50–150 vehicles, delivery/last-mile, 2–3 locations, 6–15 vendors,
six of seven answered No or Not sure.

> **Subject: Your fleet assessment — Northline Delivery**
>
> Marcus — I read through your assessment.
>
> You answered six of the seven questions with a No or a Not sure. That puts you in what I'd call
> no-baseline territory: not that the fleet is badly run, but that you don't currently have the
> data to know what it's costing you. Most fleets I see at 50–150 vehicles are in exactly this
> spot, because the systems that worked fine at 40 trucks quietly stop working somewhere around 80.
>
> Two things stand out, and in a fleet your size they're usually where the money is:
>
> You're buying maintenance at retail. No negotiated labor rates, no preferred network, and
> somewhere between six and fifteen shops in the mix. Fleets that consolidate to a preferred
> network with negotiated rates routinely take 18–32% off the average repair cost for identical
> work.
>
> You can't see your own cost. No cost-per-vehicle figure and no spend forecast means every budget
> conversation is an estimate, and overspend stays invisible because there's nothing to measure it
> against. It's also why the first problem is hard to fix — you can't negotiate a rate you can't
> benchmark.
>
> What I'd do first: establish the baseline, then renegotiate, in that order. Fixing vendor rates
> before you know your own numbers gets you a discount off a price you never validated.
>
> Worth 30 minutes to walk through what I'd look at first? No pitch — you'll leave with the list
> either way, and if it turns out you can run this internally, that's a perfectly good outcome. You
> can grab a time straight from my calendar:
>
> https://outlook.office.com/book/ApexFleetConsulting2@apexfleetconsulting.com/
>
> Brooke Benkert
> Apex Fleet Consulting
> Independent. Experienced. Results-focused.

### The benchmark library (whitepaper-sourced, used for scored post-assessment replies)

Every figure below is already published in Apex's own whitepaper. **Cite these; invent nothing.** A
number that can't be sourced is a number a fleet director will ask to be sourced.

| When the gap is | The line you can use |
|---|---|
| No negotiated rates / no vendor network (Q4, Q6) | Consolidation to a preferred network with negotiated rates routinely takes 18–32% off average repair cost for comparable work. Vendor optimization typically reduces total vendor spend 8–18%, cost recovered in 60–90 days. |
| No PM schedule / can't report compliance (Q1, Q2) | Reactive fleets typically run under 60% PM compliance against an 85–95% target. A reactive repair event costs 3–5× the planned equivalent. |
| Downtime not root-caused (Q5) | Unplanned events average 2–4 days out of service; the same work planned averages 4–8 hours. |
| No cost baseline / no forecast (Q3, Q7) | Reactive fleets can't forecast spend at all; structured programs forecast to ±10%. Without a baseline, overspend is undetectable — nothing to measure against. |
| Comparable outcome — small fleet | 85-vehicle HVAC contractor: PM compliance 41% → 89% in six months, unplanned downtime events −34%, repair cost per event −22%, ~$210,000 annualized reduction. |
| Comparable outcome — mid fleet | 180-vehicle delivery operation: 14 → 6 preferred vendors, labor rate −19%, PM compliance 58% → 91%, fleet availability 87% → 94%. |

### What stays out of this email

- **No price.** The number waits for the call to confirm asset mix and annual spend. A figure sent
  on partial data either underprices the work or scares off a real lead, and can't be walked back.
- **No proposal attached.** The proposal follows the call, scoped to one phase. Attaching it now
  says the answers weren't actually read.
- **Not all seven failures.** Two, with what each costs. Listing every gap reads as an indictment.
- **No vendor names.** Provider sourcing is a service sold inside an engagement, not a free
  introduction — and toll/citation vendors stay out entirely until the scope carve-out is settled.

### Booking form questions

A cold booking bypasses the assessment, so the booking form carries the routing minimum on its own.
Four required, one optional, in this order (Bookings won't let a custom field sit inside the
built-in name/email block, so first-in-the-custom-block is as high as any field can go):

| Question | Type | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| Company name | Text, required | Contact record and the pipeline card name |
| How many vehicles are in your fleet? | Choice — Under 50 / 50–150 / 150–500 / 500+ | Gate 3 tier test, Uptech 500+ trigger, both pricing frameworks — same buckets as the assessment |
| Which state(s) do you operate in? | Text, required | Gate 1 — without this the call can't be routed before it happens |
| Have you completed the online assessment? | Choice — Yes / No | Whether a score already exists before the meeting |
| What's the main thing you'd like to cover? | Text, optional | Prep — often the most useful field on the form |

### When a booking arrives with no assessment behind it

It bypassed the tracked front door, so three things happen by hand — all quick at this volume, and
this is exactly the job for Symphony's Scheduler role (`SKILL.md` §9) on any cold-outreach-driven
booking:

1. **Run the gates from the booking answers.** Company, vehicle count, and states are enough to
   route SVT vs. Apex before the call.
2. **Add them to Wix Contacts and create the New Inquiry pipeline card manually.** Nothing upstream
   did it automatically the way an assessment submission does.
3. **Send the assessment link ahead of the call** — "if you have five minutes before we talk, this
   makes the call a lot sharper." Most will do it, and the call starts with a score instead of cold.

## Step 5 — Record it, whichever way it routed

A decision that isn't written down looks, months later, like business quietly diverted. **The Wix
automation already creates a New Inquiry pipeline card on every assessment submission**, so the
card exists before the reply email is even opened.

| Outcome | Log where | The line that matters |
|---|---|---|
| Apex | Apex pipeline + Routing Decisions | Name the failing gate. e.g. "Michigan, outside the service footprint, no Exhibit A objective." |
| SVT | Deal Pipeline + Objectives & Evidence | Objective, tier, and honest source. Exhibit A pays on conversion, not sourcing — accurate source logging costs nothing. |
| Uptech | Appended to the Apex record | Registration date, Uptech's written confirmation, and the day-150 renewal reminder. |
| Hold | Routing Decisions | The single open question and who answers it — usually Mike for SVT, Cenk for Uptech. |

### The three things the form still can't tell you

- **Asset mix** — single class or mixed power. Drives a full point on the audit score.
- **Annual maintenance spend** — drives the VNO score and the Enterprise PMA gross-profit test.
  Deliberately not on the form: asking a stranger their budget on step one costs completions.
- **Contract state** — whether existing vendor agreements are formal and active. Changes both the
  score and whether renegotiation is billable scope.

All three are natural discovery-call questions — the reason the assessment is a conversation
starter, not an instant quote.

## Admin notes (for whoever manages the Bookings page, not for outbound agents)

- **Deleting a booking page:** Bookings has no delete option in its own UI (Clone/Share only).
  Deletion is a Microsoft 365 admin action: admin center → Users → Active users (booking pages
  appear as mailboxes), or Exchange Online PowerShell:
  ```
  Get-EXOMailbox -RecipientTypeDetails SchedulingMailbox | Select DisplayName, PrimarySmtpAddress
  Remove-Mailbox -Identity <alias>
  ```
  Permanent, no recovery window. PowerShell is safer when two pages share a display name, since it
  shows the addresses before acting.
- **Domain fix history:** the page originally minted on `pawsnrelaxpetcare.com` because the
  tenant's `BookingsMailboxDomain` was explicitly pointed there (not visible in any admin UI, not a
  default-domain fallback). One Exchange Online PowerShell command repointed it; a clone of the
  original page picked up the new domain immediately, carrying over service/staff/questions intact.
